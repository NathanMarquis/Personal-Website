The short version:

I build systems that other people depend on, and I take the “depend on” part seriously.

Background

I’m a computer science senior at Boise State University with a cybersecurity emphasis, and my favorite problem has always been the one nobody sees when things go right. My first “production system” was my own home server: a Proxmox box running photo archives, file sync, and self-hosted applications for my family, with encrypted offsite backups and automated security patching. It taught me more about reliability than any course, because when it fails, the failure is personal.

Before school I served five years in the U.S. Marine Corps as an aviation maintenance technician, inspecting and repairing a 28-helicopter fleet, then running daily operations for a 40+ person team. That’s where the habits came from: measure before you claim, document as you go, assume the network is hostile, and make every change reversible. I’m looking for roles where that mindset matters: systems, security, or infrastructure engineering.

How I work

🔑

Reversibility first

Snapshots before changes, logical dumps over raw files, every deploy with a rollback path.

👁

Document as you go

My infrastructure doc exists because I forgot things. Write it down the day you learn it, not the week you need it.

🛡

Assume breach

Everything network-reachable is attack surface. Least privilege, scoped grants, and no exceptions for convenience.

📈

Verify, don’t hope

A change isn’t done until logs and a live health check say so. “It should work” is not a conclusion.
